# Veylora RIP Product Requirements Document

**Status:** Draft PRD for project build  
**Date:** November 7, 2026  
**Owner:** Project author  
**Product:** Veylora RIP  
**Positioning:** Native control for every printed layer.

## 1. Executive summary

Veylora RIP is a native Windows direct-to-film RIP for one converted Epson ET-8550 configuration. It will generate its own printer-ready output, including independently controllable process and white channels, without requiring Digital Factory, AcroRIP, PrintFab, or another commercial RIP at runtime.

The first user is the project owner producing personal high-quality DTF transfers. The architecture should be suitable for future distribution, but the first release is not a commercial platform, cloud service, or broad printer ecosystem. The first product milestone is a repeatable pressed transfer made from bytes generated by Veylora RIP.

Digital Factory and AcroRIP are benchmark references. Digital Factory informs white controls, calibration, raw-plane inspection, gang sheets, portable jobs, and production recovery. AcroRIP informs model-specific printer configuration, ink sequencing, media options, feed behavior, and page placement. Their advertised capabilities are inspiration and comparison points, not proof of compatibility or output parity.

## 2. Problem statement

A converted consumer printer can physically produce DTF transfers, but the workflow depends on an opaque combination of tank mapping, printer protocol, media handling, device screening, white separation, color calibration, film finishing, and heat pressing. Commercial RIPs hide much of this complexity and may not expose the independent control or reproducibility desired by the project owner.

Without a native, inspectable workflow:

- the logical white plane may not correspond to the physical white slots;
- changing a model setting may affect feed, placement, or channel order unexpectedly;
- a preview may not represent the final screened device raster;
- a successful spool operation may not mean a usable transfer was printed;
- reprints may silently use changed recipes or create duplicates after a crash;
- color and white quality cannot be tied to a named, measured print condition.

Veylora RIP exists to make the separation, device configuration, output stream, and production history explicit and testable.

## 3. Product vision

Enable a skilled operator to move from transparent artwork to a measured, repeatable, native DTF transfer while being able to inspect and explain every meaningful transformation.

A high-end result means:

- correct physical dimensions and film orientation;
- controlled CMYK and white deposition;
- predictable edges, ramps, fine detail, and opacity;
- measured color for a named ink, film, mode, and finishing recipe;
- recoverable jobs and trustworthy reprints;
- diagnostic evidence when the result is wrong.

High-end does not mean supporting every printer, every file format, or every commercial feature in the first release.

## 4. Users

### Primary user: owner/operator

A technically capable individual who owns or controls a converted ET-8550, performs artwork preparation and printing personally, and is willing to run channel tests, calibration targets, and physical transfer experiments.

**Needs:** native control, inspectability, repeatability, clear diagnostics, safe experimentation, and high-quality output.

**Constraints:** may have one printer, one conversion map, limited equipment, and no established physical tolerances at project start.

### Future user: small-shop operator

An operator who needs reliable daily output without understanding printer protocol internals.

**Needs:** validated presets, preflight, simple queue states, portable jobs, selective reprints, labels, and troubleshooting guidance.

This user is a future validation target. The first release must not pretend to support shop-scale production until physical and operational tests support that claim.

### Future distributor/support maintainer

A person who installs Veylora RIP on another Windows machine or supports a different verified device configuration.

**Needs:** clean installation, documented compatibility, setup verification, portable diagnostics, schema migration, license clarity, and safe updates.

## 5. Goals

### Product goals

1. Prove native printer output on one converted ET-8550 configuration.
2. Make process and white planes independently controllable and inspectable.
3. Tie output to immutable device setup, print mode, calibration, profile, and finishing records.
4. Produce repeatable transfers with a clear recovery path after failures.
5. Provide a useful personal workflow before adding commercial-scale automation.
6. Build distribution-ready boundaries without requiring accounts, cloud services, or payment infrastructure.

### Engineering goals

- Separate source import, layout, separation, calibration, screening, encoding, transport, and UI.
- Use a file sink and final-output inspector before relying on native submission.
- Render large sheets through bounded, cancellable bands.
- Reject unsupported inputs and incompatible recipes before submission.
- Preserve exact job and attempt artifacts where configured.

### Learning goals

- Determine whether the converted ET-8550 can be driven natively with the required independent channel control.
- Determine a validated ink map, print mode, media path, screening approach, and white allocation policy.
- Establish measurable quality and repeatability baselines against a legitimate commercial reference where available.

## 6. Non-goals for the first release

The first release will not attempt to:

- support a broad printer family;
- support rolls, multi-lane production, or industrial throughput;
- reproduce every Digital Factory or AcroRIP feature;
- provide a full vector design editor;
- build a generic N-color ICC profile wizard;
- support fluorescent, varnish, or UV channels;
- automate printer cleaning, firmware changes, or waste-counter operations;
- provide cloud queues, accounts, payments, subscriptions, DRM, or remote telemetry;
- guarantee color or wash durability across unmeasured garments and finishing recipes;
- claim native support before the ET-8550 backend passes physical gates.

## 7. Product principles

1. **Native means no commercial RIP dependency.** Commercial tools may serve as references.
2. **One verified configuration beats many unverified settings.**
3. **Logical channels are not physical slots.**
4. **The final raster is the product truth.** Previews must identify which stage they represent.
5. **Every transform has one owner.** Mirror, scaling, profiles, white generation, screening, and encoding cannot happen twice.
6. **Physical evidence outranks plausible software output.**
7. **Jobs are immutable and attempts are append-only.**
8. **Unsafe ambiguity becomes a review state.** Never silently retry a job that may have printed.
9. **Unsupported input fails clearly.** Silent approximations are unacceptable for a high-end workflow.
10. **Personal-first does not mean disposable.** Use versioned schemas, portable jobs, dependency records, and diagnostics from the beginning.

## 8. Core user workflows

### Workflow A: establish a device configuration

1. Operator records printer identity, firmware observation, conversion supplier, physical tank map, ink, film, feed path, and reference software settings.
2. Veylora creates an unverified device setup.
3. Operator prints a labeled channel target through the available reference path or native probe.
4. Operator identifies logical and physical channels, including white slots.
5. Veylora stores the verified map and prevents silent changes.

**Exit:** every required channel is physically identified and the setup is versioned.

### Workflow B: prove the native backend

1. Veylora renders a bounded diagnostic target.
2. Operator inspects logical planes, physical-slot rasters, and screened output.
3. Veylora writes a file artifact and, when ready, submits it through the native transport.
4. Operator prints the target on the chosen film.
5. The target is inspected on film and after pressing.
6. Results and observations are attached to the mode and recipe.

**Exit:** independent K and W behavior, overlap order, orientation, scale, feed, and repeatability are physically demonstrated.

### Workflow C: prepare a production sheet

1. Operator imports supported transparent artwork.
2. Veylora reads profiles, alpha, dimensions, and metadata.
3. Operator places, sizes, rotates, duplicates, and optionally imports a white mask.
4. Veylora generates CMYK and white planes under the selected recipe.
5. Operator reviews garment simulation, film orientation, white plane, clear mask, and final screened output.
6. Preflight blocks invalid dimensions, incompatible recipes, unsupported inputs, and coverage violations.
7. The job snapshot is saved before rendering.

**Exit:** a native-ready artifact exists with a complete, reproducible snapshot.

### Workflow D: submit and recover

1. Operator inspects the retained artifact.
2. Veylora records a submission attempt.
3. A single device sender writes the native stream.
4. Veylora tracks submission and spool evidence separately from physical completion.
5. A timeout, disconnect, or restart creates an ambiguous review state rather than an automatic retry.
6. Operator confirms physical result or creates a linked reprint attempt.

**Exit:** no silent duplicate and complete attempt history.

### Workflow E: calibrate a high-quality recipe

1. Operator selects a stable mode and physical condition.
2. Veylora generates channel, white, wet-load, and color targets.
3. External measurement tools or instruments provide results.
4. Veylora stores calibration curves, profile references, white policy, screen settings, and finishing conditions.
5. Holdout patches are printed and measured.
6. The recipe is marked experimental, validated, or superseded.

**Exit:** a named recipe has reproducible settings and evidence-based tolerances.

## 9. Functional requirements

### P0: native feasibility and correctness

- Native file sink and device backend boundary.
- Device setup with logical-to-physical mapping.
- Versioned PrintMode and backend capability model.
- Bounded renderer with deterministic band behavior.
- PNG SDR import with alpha/profile metadata.
- Physical layout, printable bounds, orientation, and one mirror owner.
- CMYK and white separation with independent supplied masks.
- Shared knockout/perforation clear mask.
- Deterministic screening and channel/white limits.
- Logical, physical-slot, and screened-raster inspection.
- Diagnostic target generation.
- Native submission lifecycle with ambiguous-attempt protection.

### P1: personal production workflow

- Manual layout, duplication, quantities, gutters, and transparency-aware crop.
- Portable job bundles with assets and hashes.
- Preflight and compatibility checks.
- White curves, highlight policy, choke/spread, and white-slot allocation.
- Calibration records, linearization, and compatible profile import.
- Queue hold/release/cancel, RIP-only inspection, and selective reprint.
- Crash recovery, disk-full recovery, and diagnostic reports.
- Repeated physical quality tests for one validated recipe.

### P2: future distribution and production expansion

- Clean installer and supported-machine setup wizard.
- Schema migration, backup/restore, signed updates, and rollback.
- PDF/SVG/TIFF input after renderer fidelity testing.
- Named colors, spot channels, and richer vector workflows.
- Automatic nesting, barcode/hot-folder workflows, labels, costing, and broader devices.
- Additional backends only after the device capability boundary is proven.

## 10. Quality and success metrics

Metrics must be baselined against the actual converted printer and selected finishing process. The following are measurement requirements, not assumed targets.

### Native feasibility metrics

- **Channel independence:** isolated K, W, and required CMY patches produce only the intended physical channels.
- **Orientation correctness:** asymmetric marker, ruler, and text appear with the intended film and garment orientation.
- **Registration:** process/white offset measured at center and corners across repeated sheets.
- **Scale accuracy:** printed rulers and known dimensions compared with requested physical dimensions.
- **Repeatability:** same retained job repeated across sessions with documented variance.
- **Recovery safety:** ambiguous submission never creates an automatic duplicate.

### Quality metrics

- White opacity and edge halo assessment against the chosen reference.
- Channel linearity and maximum usable coverage.
- Combined wet-load behavior and pooling/adhesion defects.
- Color error and repeatability on holdout patches under recorded measurement conditions.
- Fine-detail survival, gradient smoothness, banding, moiré, and dot gain.
- Pressed-transfer stretch, peel, and wash behavior for tested controls.

### Workflow metrics

- Time from imported artwork to inspected native-ready artifact.
- Number of blocking preflight errors caught before submission.
- Reprint time for a selected placement.
- Percentage of jobs with complete device, recipe, and artifact provenance.
- Time to diagnose a known failure using the diagnostic report.
- Spoilage and duplicate-print incidents during representative personal use.

Do not set universal pass thresholds before the reference baseline exists. Freeze thresholds after baseline measurement and before judging the candidate output.

## 11. Release strategy and gates

### Gate 0: preparation complete

**Required:** feature requirements, device test plan, supplier/ink/film information, safe workspace, reference workflow, and artifact-recording procedure.

**Not met if:** the physical conversion map, intended media, or finishing process is unknown.

### Gate 1: native device proof

**Required:** native probe, verified channel mapping, bounded test stream, independent K/W behavior, orientation, feed, scale, and repeated pressed transfer.

**Not met if:** success depends on a commercial RIP or only a spooler success response.

### Gate 2: rendering correctness

**Required:** deterministic source import, white policy, masks, transforms, screening, bounded bands, and final-output inspection.

**Not met if:** banded output differs unexpectedly, hidden RGB leaks, white changes process coverage unintentionally, or final transformations cannot be inspected.

### Gate 3: personal production readiness

**Required:** one validated mode and recipe, preflight, portable jobs, queue recovery, selective reprint, and repeated quality evidence.

**Not met if:** output quality is only visually acceptable once or job recovery can duplicate output silently.

### Gate 4: distribution-ready beta

**Required:** clean installation, compatibility matrix, schema migrations, support bundles, safe archive handling, release/update process, dependency/license inventory, and tested setup wizard.

**Not met if:** another machine cannot install, inspect, or safely reject incompatible jobs.

These gates are sequential. UI polish, nesting, and automation do not substitute for Gate 1 or Gate 2.

## 12. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation / decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ET-8550 native protocol is incomplete or inaccessible | Project cannot meet native requirement | Prove native channel addressing first. Bound investigation and document the blocker rather than redefining prepress as success. |
| Conversion map differs from vendor documentation | Wrong ink or unsafe output | Require physical labeled channel test and versioned setup. |
| White slots are not independently addressable | White quality or allocation assumptions fail | Test physical slots before implementing production white policy. |
| Media feed, film sensing, or page origin is unstable | Spoilage and registration failure | Treat feed, page size, guides, and mode as one validated bundle. |
| Color engine/profile does not model white and garment result | Misleading color claims | Freeze a recipe and white policy, use holdouts, label unprofiled paths. |
| Commercial feature comparisons create scope explosion | Long build with no proven output | One ET-8550 mode first; defer broad devices, cloud, automation, and advanced format support. |
| Native stream works digitally but transfer quality fails | Software appears finished but is unusable | Press and measure physical controls at every release gate. |
| Crash or timeout causes duplicate | Material waste and operator mistrust | Durable attempts, ambiguous review state, no automatic retry. |
| Future distribution introduces legal/security issues | Release blocked or unsafe | Track licenses, profiles, fonts, archives, installer, updates, and diagnostics from the start. |
| Feature controls become misleading | Operators believe unsupported settings work | Capability-driven UI and preflight blocking. |

## 13. Dependencies and assumptions

### Dependencies

- Converted ET-8550 and actual physical tank/slot map.
- Intended film, ink, powder, press, cure, peel, and wash process.
- Windows print computer and native transport access.
- Legitimate reference RIP or driver where available for comparison.
- Measurement instrument or profiling service for high-end color claims.
- Rust rendering and image/color libraries selected with license review.
- Local disk capacity for retained rasters and printer-ready artifacts.

### Assumptions to verify

- The conversion can accept the desired ink configuration without unsafe maintenance consequences.
- The printer stream can provide independent process/white control in at least one useful mode.
- The chosen film can feed reliably through the selected path.
- The physical white and process order produces a usable pressed transfer.
- The owner accepts a native feasibility investigation before building the full editor.
- The selected Windows print path exposes enough control and status for safe operation.

## 14. Open decisions

These must be resolved by evidence or an explicit project decision:

1. Exact physical ink map and whether both white slots are usable.
2. Native protocol family and command framing.
3. First validated print mode, resolution, feed path, and printable box.
4. Whether screening is application-owned, device-owned, or split.
5. White-slot allocation and calibration method.
6. Color-management library and external profile workflow.
7. Supported PNG subset and policy for TIFF/PDF/SVG expansion.
8. Retention policy for large raster and stream artifacts.
9. Measurement instrument or profiling service access.
10. Quality thresholds after commercial-reference baseline testing.

These are not reasons to block the PRD. They are explicit discovery and Gate 1/Gate 2 work.

## 15. Product decisions recorded

- Product name: **Veylora RIP**.
- Initial user: project owner/operator.
- Initial platform: Windows desktop.
- Initial device: one converted Epson ET-8550.
- Product mode: native RIP, not a commercial-RIP wrapper.
- Commercial RIPs: references only, never runtime requirements.
- Architecture: headless rendering/backend core with a desktop application boundary.
- Initial input: SDR PNG with transparency plus aligned grayscale white masks.
- Initial media: one validated cut-sheet film configuration.
- Future distribution: design for it, but defer storefront infrastructure.
