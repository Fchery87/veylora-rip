# A Rust DTF RIP for the Epson ET-8550

Research and engineering plan · November 7, 2026

> Direction update: the target is now explicitly a native RIP with no commercial RIP runtime dependency, for personal high-end work and possible future distribution. See the [Digital Factory / AcroRIP comparison and native-first roadmap](native-rip-competitive-gap-analysis.md) for prioritized additions and revised acceptance gates. Commercial RIP handoff below is an optional development aid, not the product's success criterion.

Build the application around a single converted ET-8550 with cut-sheet film. Develop the artwork and separation pipeline alongside a small printer experiment. The first hardware milestone is a repeatable pressed transfer made from bytes our software generated, with independently controlled process black and white. Start with one confirmed ink map, film, and print mode. Windows and USB are proposed starting points because the workspace is on Windows and there are documented commercial USB workflows.

The [DTF RIP market research](dtf-rip-market-research.md) compares the six commercial tools that informed this plan.

## What the printer evidence says

Epson sells the ET-8550 as a six-ink photo printer. Its factory slots are Black (BK), Photo Black (PB), Gray (GY), Cyan, Magenta, and Yellow. The US spec identifies ESC/P-R and lists USB, Ethernet, and Wi-Fi. The 13 × 19 inch photo-paper spec is a starting point for sheet presets. Actual film margins, origin, and reliable feeding depend on the conversion, driver, and media. Epson distinguishes the rear feeder from the single-sheet rear feed slot. [Epson ink list](https://epson.com/i/C11CJ21201?IC01=K%2CNA&IC02=K%2CNA&IC03=C%2CNA&IC04=Y%2CNA&IC05=M%2CNA&IC06=LK%2CNA&ICNUM=6) · [Specification sheet](https://mediaserver.goepson.com/ImConvServlet/imconv/a3af1cb8cd8ac0b5c4f40ffa3c24c1371b646b10/original?assetDescr=EcoTank_ET-8550_Device_Specification_Sheet_CPD-59933R5) · [ET-8550 user guide](https://files.support.epson.com/docid/cpd5/cpd59879.pdf)

| Commercial reference | What it documents |
| --- | --- |
| [PrintFab DTF](https://www.zedonet.com/en_support_dtf.phtml) | The standard ET-8550 driver supports white in Gray. Its separate [ET-8550 RIP driver](https://www.printfab.com/en/printer_Epson_EcoTank_ET8550_RIP.html) lists white in BK and PB and process black in Gray. |
| [AcroRIP desktopDTF](https://www.acrorip.com/supported-printers.php) | Lists the ET-8550 and L8180. Its [release notes](https://acrorip.com/updates.php) document an ET-8500/8550 ink sequence option and model-specific fixes for film recognition, rear feed, and page placement. Treat the sequence as vendor notation until decoded against the actual slot labels. |
| [Fiery Digital Factory ET-8550 V2](https://help.fiery.com/FieryDigitalFactory/ReadMe/Epson_ET8550_v2_en_US.pdf) | Documents configurable ink order, a labeled quick test, and a Thin Paper setting for its own queue. The notes also cover incomplete jobs, jams, and white-ink maintenance. |

Before filling the printer for the conversion, reconcile the conversion supplier's slot instructions with the exact reference RIP driver and mode. Record the tank labels and fill map, firmware, print path, film, and configuration version. Verify the map physically with individual test patches. Store logical ink (C, M, Y, K, W), physical slots (BK, PB, GY, C, M, Y), and protocol channel identifiers as separate concepts. If two slots carry white, split or otherwise calibrate coverage for the pair. Sending the full white value to each would make total coverage dependent on the number of white slots.

### Where to look for an independent backend

| Path | Evidence and next step |
| --- | --- |
| Commercial RIP as a reference | Use a legitimate ET-8550 driver to establish a known-good print. PrintFab Composer documents transparent PNG/TIFF, alpha or threshold-driven white, separate light/dark white settings, and white-on-top. A manual transparent-sheet handoff could get Rust layout into production early. Confirm mirror ownership and actual import behavior. Independent white-plane import, job submission automation, and control over device halftoning are open interoperability questions. [PrintFab DTF workflow](https://www.zedonet.com/en_support_dtf.phtml) |
| Native Windows transport | A RAW spool job carries bytes already in the printer's language. `WritePrinter` is a transport candidate once the encoder works. Ordinary OS printing of a PNG, PDF, or RGB raster has no independently controlled white plane in the workflow we have verified. Fiery documents output-to-file controls and a separate visual plane inspector. Verify the file produced by the chosen driver before treating it as a reference device stream. [Microsoft WritePrinter](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/printdocs/writeprinter) · [Fiery production interface](https://help.cadlink.com/website/digital_factory/en/production/vpm_interface.htm) · [Fiery plane inspection](https://help.cadlink.com/website/digital_factory/en/bonus_documentation/view_raw_data.htm) |
| Legacy ESC/P2 experiment | A TU Delft team reported generating ESC/P2 jobs for the related ET-8500 and printing grids with all six factory inks. The experiments used the CD-tray path and also reported dot-placement and merging limitations. This gives us a concrete command family to investigate alongside the ESC/P-R named in Epson's ET-8550 spec. ET-8550 film feed, pass order, and production quality still need hardware proof. ### First bench experiment

1. **Establish a reference.** Record printer firmware, exact physical ink map, driver version, media source, mode, and film. On the conversion, check nozzle health and print a small labeled channel target through the commercial RIP. Confirm actual K, each configured W slot where independently addressable, and CMY. An isolated white mark must land with no process color and an isolated K mark must land with no white.
2. **Get comparable jobs.** Where the applicable terms permit, export or capture reference output from our own test artwork. Start with a blank, one narrow mark, an X/Y shift, and a change of one plane at a time while holding size and mode fixed. Then vary media size, feed source, and print direction separately. Save settings and observations alongside each sample. If file output is unavailable, USBPcap is a Windows capture option. [Wireshark USB capture notes](https://wiki.wireshark.org/CaptureSetup/USB/)
3. **Prove the command path.** Identify job framing, raster encoding, color selection, origin, feed and eject behavior, and the mode's interleaving or pass requirements. Try a tiny bounded sheet with one generated process patch and one white patch before larger jobs. Add an overlapping CMYK/white patch and asymmetric text. Confirm layer order on film and correct orientation after transfer. Correct ink order in a raster buffer alone says little about the order ink lands on film.
4. **Prove registration and repeatability.** Print a ruler, registration crosses, fine type, white ramps, and a near-black ramp at different points on the same sheet. Press the target onto a dark garment. Repeat on fresh film after a restart and compare registration, scale, margins, adhesion, and white density to the commercial reference. Record actual hardware status available during paper-out, partial send, and cancellation.
5. **Decide the backend.** Continue toward native production once we can repeatedly address the required slots and match the reference's usable area, orientation, and layer registration in one mode. If native output remains incomplete, keep the manual handoff useful for gang sheets while retaining per-plane export for development. Full white tuning and device screening still require a verified backend.

The DTF workflow prints mirrored color against the film and white behind it, then applies and cures adhesive powder before pressing the transfer. White has two jobs to validate on the garment: optical backing and support for powder pickup in the tested process. Store the supplier's film, powder, cure, press, and peel conditions with sample results. Epson's F1070 guide is a process and troubleshooting reference for a purpose-built printer. Use the conversion supplier's instructions for the ET-8550. [PrintFab layer order](https://www.zedonet.com/en_support_dtf.phtml) · [Epson DTF film guide](https://files.support.epson.com/docid/other/cmp0437-00_en.pdf)

## What the Rust application should own

```text
Artwork and source color metadata
  -> placement, scale, and transparency-aware compositing
  -> garment knockout and perforation mask generation
  -> CMYK conversion and white separation
  -> enforce shared clear areas after white edge adjustments
  -> calibrated coverage, physical ink mapping, and screening
  -> validated device encoding and transport
```

Keep previews and production output tied to the same placement transforms and separation settings. Use garment view for editing and a distinct film view for the mirrored result. Apply mirror at one explicit point in the pipeline and apply it to every plane and printed mark together. A vendor adapter that owns mirroring receives unmirrored artwork. Test text and an off-center marker to catch a second mirror or a shifted origin.

### White generation and transparency

- Start with alpha as the coverage mask. Fully transparent pixels produce zero ink by default even if their hidden RGB is saturated. An explicit white spread can extend the backing past a source edge, while an intentional knockout or perforation stays clear. Opaque white artwork must be eligible for white ink even when its CMYK is zero. Warn on an opaque background and offer a preview of intentional removal. Preserve embedded ICC data and unassociated alpha on PNG import. Normalize inputs into a shared linear-light working space, premultiply for interpolation and compositing, then recover straight color for color transforms where coverage is nonzero. [W3C PNG specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/png/)
- Offer white density, an independent highlight white response, and white under shadows. Use a ramp and printed edge samples to tune partial alpha. Provide signed choke/spread in millimeters, evaluated at output scale. Choke shrinks the backing to reduce visible rims. Strong choke can erase hairlines and remove backing that fine details need. Show what changed in the white-only view and allow inspection at print scale. Treat vendor percentages as mode-specific starting points. [Fiery V2 notes](https://help.fiery.com/FieryDigitalFactory/ReadMe/Epson_ET8550_v2_en_US.pdf) · [PrintFab DTF controls](https://www.zedonet.com/en_support_dtf.phtml)
- Keep three views available before release to print: simulated garment composite, white coverage, and final film orientation. A garment composite is an approximation until backed by physical calibration. Show the actual generated separation for decisions about edges, holes, and white density. [Fiery raw plane viewer](https://help.cadlink.com/website/digital_factory/en/bonus_documentation/view_raw_data.htm)

### Knockout, perforation, and device screening

| Operation | Expected behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Garment-color knockout | Remove selected colors or near-black regions from the design so the shirt supplies that tone. Use tolerance, a transition control, and a garment-color preview. Clear the corresponding process and white coverage. Use as an explicit per-design edit because a black knockout prepared for a black tee can look wrong on navy or white. [Fiery KnockMeOut documentation](https://help.cadlink.com/website/digital_factory/en/bonus_documentation/knockmeout.htm) |
| Perforation for hand feel | Make deliberate clear holes in selected solid areas. Use one shared mask across all printed layers, apply it after any white spread that could fill holes, and enforce the clear region through screening. Control physical hole size, spacing, and eligible area. Protect small letters and thin edges. Compare an unperforated control with several candidates on the same garment after pressing, stretch, and laundering. |
| Device halftoning | Convert continuous ink coverage into dot or drop levels the chosen mode supports. Begin with a deterministic threshold screen if the native encoder requires screened rasters. Evaluate uniform ramps, grain, moiré, band seams, and dot gain before adding alternative algorithms. Keep screen phase in page coordinates for banded rendering. Vendor handoff delegates device screening to the vendor RIP. |

### Color management and usable presets

**First release.** Read tagged source profiles, use an explicit sRGB assumption for untagged PNGs, and allow a verified output profile to be imported. Expose rendering intent and modest, non-destructive edits. Keep source, proof display, and device transforms distinct. A Little CMS binding is a practical candidate for native transforms. Keep white as a separately calibrated spot plane for the initial CMYK workflow. An output profile must match the backend input color space and calibrated print condition. When a vendor RIP owns the output transform, send supported source artwork with its profile and apply output conversion only once. [ICC specifications](https://www.color.org/specifications/) · [Little CMS](https://github.com/mm2/Little-CMS) · [Rust binding](https://docs.rs/crate/lcms2/latest)

**Build a measured preset.** Lock the ink set, physical map, film, print mode and resolution, screening, white policy, and finishing conditions. Establish single-channel limits, linearization, and a combined wet-ink ceiling with test ramps and overprints. Check pooling, fine detail, and adhesion before using the condition for profiling. Print known chart values through the same calibration and screening with output ICC conversion disabled. Measure pressed charts on a consistent garment and backing, record the measurement condition, and build a profile for that recipe. For a native CMYK chart, ArgyllCMS supplies calibration, target, instrument, and profile tools. Its `colprof` supports RGB, CMY and CMYK. Profile checks should include separately printed holdout patches as well as visual neutrals, deep blacks, skin tones, and saturated artwork. [ArgyllCMS workflow](https://www.argyllcms.com/doc/ArgyllDoc.html) · [Linearization](https://www.argyllcms.com/doc/printcal.html) · [Measurement](https://www.argyllcms.com/doc/chartread.html) · [Profile verification](https://www.argyllcms.com/doc/profcheck.html)

**Package measured conditions.** Separate a Device Setup (identity, ink map, transport) from a Print Recipe (media, mode, bounds, calibration, output profile, and defaults). Store garment-dependent artwork settings separately. Make profile changes and overrides visible in the job record. Include an output ICC profile with the app once it has been validated for a named recipe and redistribution is cleared. Factory photo-paper profiles characterize a different printing condition.

### Gang sheets and shop workflow

- **Design and layout.** Drag in transparent artwork, set its physical dimensions, lock aspect ratio, rotate, snap, and duplicate with shared source assets. Warn on low effective PPI, clipping, accidental overlap, and content outside the validated printable box. Set gutters for hand cutting and show sheet use. Store positions in physical units independently of screen pixels and device DPI. Start with manual placement and duplication. Add deterministic rectangle packing once manual layout works, respecting gutters and allowed rotation. Offer tight shape nesting and roll handling after cut-sheet production is dependable.
- **Proof and production.** Preflight before submission. Show which device, media, ink map, and recipe the sheet will use. Offer a small sample crop at original scale, per-plane inspection, and a job label inside reserved margins. Snapshot the assets, layout, recipe, profile hash, and engine version when queuing. Preserve the original output for exact reprints. A recipe change creates a new run.
- **Queue semantics.** Use a single sender per device and durable states for draft, held, rendering, ready, submitting, submitted, needs review, and operator-confirmed finished. Priorities affect ready jobs. Persist a submission attempt and correlate a spooler ID where available. Treat ambiguous submission after a crash or disconnect as needs review before allowing a reprint. Distinguish successful spooling from a usable transfer. Reprints get a new attempt and retain the original snapshot. [Windows spooler job operations](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/print/functions-defined-by-print-providers)
- **Troubleshooting.** Start with printable diagnostic targets, timestamped nozzle checks, and a compact job report. For missing white, inspect the raster first, then slot map, selected mode, and nozzle health. For halos, compare digital choke with physical registration. For banding, compare the nozzle target and feed/direction settings before changing color. For poor adhesion, compare white coverage and hole size against a control printed and pressed with the same supplies. Keep cleaning and ink maintenance with the operator and conversion instructions. [Fiery ET-8550 notes](https://help.fiery.com/FieryDigitalFactory/ReadMe/Epson_ET8550_v2_en_US.pdf) · [Epson film troubleshooting](https://files.support.epson.com/docid/other/cmp0437-00_en.pdf)

## Implementation sequence and acceptance gates

| Stage | Deliverable | Exit check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Device experiment | Commercial reference, small channel target, and a Rust probe that produces a bounded test job if the command path is established. | Printed process and white patches are independently controlled. The overlapping, mirrored target transfers correctly and repeats on the same setup. |
| 1. Offline prepress | Headless Rust renderer for a transparent PNG, physical sheet layout, underbase controls, white-plane diagnostics, and a transparent export for manual handoff. Run alongside stage 0. | Fixture with text, hidden RGB, alpha ramps, opaque whites, thin strokes, and known dimensions renders correctly. File handoff prints at intended scale and orientation if the chosen reference RIP supports it. |
| 2. Single-printer workflow | Desktop layout and preview, one validated recipe, backend encoding and transport, local queue, and reprint. | Multiple copies of a sheet print consistently. Paper-out, disconnect, cancellation, and restart leave a clear recovery path without an automatic duplicate. |
| 3. Quality | Measured ICC preset, white calibration, garment knockout, perforation, and proof controls. | Pressed holdout chart, registration target, and comparison garment meet tolerances agreed from the commercial baseline. Measure across repeated runs. Check the softened transfer's stretch and wash performance against its solid control. |
| 4. Production | Auto nesting and, as volume warrants, hot folders, batching, finishing labels, and additional printers or media. | Test with representative orders and compare setup time, wasted area, spoilage, and reprint accuracy. |

Start with a Rust library and small CLI. Keep layout and separation independent of the transport. Use a file sink to inspect rendered bands and a device sink for the bench test. The PNG decoder must preserve profile and transparency metadata. Use disk-backed output and bounded band buffers once needed. At 1,440 × 720 samples per inch, a 13 × 19 inch page contains about 256 million samples per plane, or about 1.28 GB for five uncompressed 8-bit planes. If a processing step needs neighboring rows, carry the required halo between bands and check that output matches an unbanded reference. Treat this sample grid as an engineering example, not a selected ET-8550 print mode. [Rust PNG library](https://github.com/image-rs/image-png) · [PNG metadata API](https://docs.rs/png/latest/png/struct.Info.html)

The bench work needs the actual conversion kit and slot map, printer firmware, intended film and finishing recipe, and a reference driver available on the print computer. This plan is based on source research. The ET-8550 device stream, film behavior, print quality, and file handoff have yet to be tested on the user's machine.
